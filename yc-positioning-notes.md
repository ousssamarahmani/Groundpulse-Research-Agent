## YC sources

The current YC Requests for Startups page is for Fall 2026 and frames the batch around AI moving into the physical world. Relevant themes include the future of American defense and data for the real world. The defense request emphasizes sensors, software, payloads, modular open-system solutions, and deployment in extreme environments. The physical-world data request emphasizes sparse sensor data, remote sensing, and building systems that model real-world operations.

Official YC application guidance emphasizes clarity, matter-of-fact product descriptions, specific founder achievements, insight rather than generic execution claims, candid acknowledgement of obstacles, and a concise application/demo. YC’s current application page says applications are accepted for future batches and that the company should apply when ready.

## Brief findings

The provided StellarOS–GroundPulse brief defines StellarOS as the long-term intelligence layer for autonomous space missions and GroundPulse as the initial ground-station intelligence product. The brief proposes monitoring, telemetry intelligence, anomaly detection, RF quality, predictive maintenance, mission copilot, a mission-control dashboard, and a phased expansion from ground stations to network operations, satellite mission intelligence, autonomous spacecraft operations, and the StellarOS platform.

Current product risk: the brief describes a strong product vision, but a YC application will need a narrow initial customer, a working data pipeline, a real operational pain point, measurable customer value, and evidence that the first wedge can expand. The strongest current narrative is not “AI for all space missions”; it is “software that helps satellite operators detect and explain ground-segment failures before they cause missed contacts.”
